GB 47955 New Standard Released: Voyager Intelligent Systems Limited Interprets New Standard Requirements via Its Technical Framework

← Back to News

On June 27, GB 47955-2026 Intelligent and connected vehicle — Safety requirements of combined driver assistance systems, China's first mandatory safety standard targeting L2-grade combined driving assistance systems, was officially promulgated. Under this new standard, advanced driver assistance systems have entered an era defined by formal safety benchmarks.

What core capabilities must a truly reliable advanced driver assistance system possess? We interviewed engineers from Voyager Intelligent Systems Limited to unpack technical challenges brought about by this new national standard.

Q1 Compared with previous industry specifications, what new core safety constraints are introduced under this new standard?

Perception Algorithm Engineer

A: This new standard establishes unified safety thresholds. Rather than merely counting available functional features, it imposes mandatory end-to-end safety requirements for complete systems, with particularly prominent updates covering perception requirements.

It also explicitly requires multi-channel redundancy for perception systems: if one sensor channel malfunctions, other sensors must continue to identify surrounding road-users and obstacles.

Furthermore, the standard raises requirements for perception modules beyond basic image capture. Systems shall implement object classification and hierarchical risk assessment, and output reliable environmental information for downstream planning and control modules.

Within the framework of this new standard, stable environmental comprehension via perception becomes a critical compliance threshold for products.

Q2 This new standard imposes strict risk-exit mechanisms for out-of-scope operating conditions and sudden hazards. What operational logic underpins Voyager Intelligent Systems Limited's planning and control framework for risk mitigation?

Planning and Control Algorithm Engineer

A: If the planning and control layer detects rising road risks, degraded sensor recognition or the vehicle drifting outside functional operating conditions, it will not abruptly disable assistance functions.

Built on Voyager Intelligent Systems Limited's proprietary VT-Vulcan® hierarchical planning and control architecture, the system executes a gradual risk-mitigation workflow: multi-stage driver alerts are triggered first, followed by gentle speed adjustment, and phased handover of vehicle control authority back to the human driver. The planning and control unit prohibits harsh vehicle maneuvers such as emergency hard braking, unintended acceleration and sharp steering-wheel rotation.

Q3 Why does this new standard mandate enhanced driver-monitoring functionality for all L2-grade vehicles?

Perception Algorithm Engineer

A: Regardless of sophistication of the ADAS, the human driver remains primarily responsible for safety, as reaffirmed within regulatory text. Vehicle design must therefore monitor both road conditions and driver states.

Voyager Intelligent Systems Limited's DMS Driver Monitoring System leverages visual perception technology to comprehensively identify driver head pose, gaze direction and hand position data, delivering robust human-machine collaboration support for advanced driver assistance systems.

Q4 What factors will define competitiveness for future advanced driver assistance systems?

Platform Architecture Engineer

A: In my opinion, future competition will no longer focus purely on the quantity of onboard functions. Instead, system completeness, stability and safety of the overall solution will constitute decisive differentiators. Voyager Intelligent Systems Limited will keep advancing its technical ecosystem covering visual perception and multi-sensor fusion positioning and navigation (VT-Sensing and VT-Navi) , and planning and control (VT-Vulcan®), to enhance overall performance of driver-assistance systems.
